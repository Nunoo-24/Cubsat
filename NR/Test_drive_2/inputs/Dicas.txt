AE CUBESAT ATTITUDE-CONTROL PROJECT — WORKING NOTES

CONFIRMED ARCHITECTURE DECISION
- The CubeSat's XIAO ESP32-S3 is the onboard computer for the autonomous system.
- Camera capture and image processing, sensor processing/fusion, attitude-control
  decisions, and reaction-wheel commands run onboard.
- The ground station is not in the autonomous feedback loop. It receives and
  records telemetry, displays housekeeping and payload data, and can send
  configuration, mode, or test commands. Autonomous pointing must continue
  without real-time ground-station commands.
- Treat ESP32-S3 compute capacity as the selected design assumption; measure
  frame rate, timing, memory, and control performance on the target hardware.

SYSTEM OVERVIEW

1. Sensing and perception (onboard)
   - Camera: Seeed XIAO ESP32-S3 Sense camera (OV5640/OV3660 variant to confirm).
   - Detect the black, high-contrast target on the poster.
   - Calculate target displacement in the camera image. Define the pixel-to-angle
     conversion and image coordinate convention; do not claim 3D position from a
     monocular image unless a depth method is added.
   - IMU: MPU-6050, 3-axis gyroscope and accelerometer.
   - LIS3MDL magnetometer is listed in the notes; its role in the first version
     remains to be decided.
   - Fuse camera's slower absolute target observation with the IMU's fast motion
     measurements as required by the controller design.

2. Attitude control and actuation (onboard)
   - Initially develop/control one free yaw axis; the other axes may be locked
     for the first simulator or bench phase if agreed with the project team.
   - Later use the project gimbal configuration: yaw is reaction-wheel
     controlled; roll and pitch are pendular/damped modes, unless the team adopts
     a full three-axis control design.
   - Motor: brushless reaction-wheel motor with moteus r4.11 controller over
     CAN-FD (exact supplied hardware to confirm).
   - The ESP32 runs the control law and sends motor commands; the wheel's angular
     momentum change rotates the cube in the opposite direction.

3. Telemetry and telecommand (CubeSat ↔ ground station)
   - Ground station PC: display and log housekeeping, IMU, target-error,
     controller/motor status, and selected payload data. Send setup, mode,
     setpoint, and test commands.
   - During autonomous target acquisition/holding/tracking, image processing and
     control decisions stay onboard. The ground station may monitor but must not
     be required to close the loop.
   - Link and packet protocol are not finalized. Wi-Fi and a simplified
     CCSDS-inspired packet format are candidate ideas, not a selected standard.
   - The current local simulator uses UDP loopback and JSON only; it is not yet
     the CubeSat radio or final TM/TC protocol.

DEVELOPMENT SEQUENCE
- Begin in simulation: telemetry link, motor/speed control, IMU estimation, and
  attitude control.
- Add target detection and camera/IMU control onboard.
- Validate through simulation, processor-in-the-loop, and hardware-in-the-loop
  as the equipment becomes available.
- Final demonstration: acquire the poster target, hold within the project
  tolerance, recover from a nudge, follow a moving target, and tolerate a brief
  camera occlusion.

Open details: camera variant, magnetometer use, initial axis constraints,
communication link/protocol, and final values/test criteria must be confirmed
against the supplied kit and the team/lecturer.
